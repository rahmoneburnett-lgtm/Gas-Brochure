Selye's General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS)

Select a stage tab below to interactively explore how long-term structural academic stress updates your body and mental systems phase-by-phase.

Stage 1: Alarm Reaction (The Shock Response)

The immediate physiological fight-or-flight shock reaction triggered by an acute stressor.

⚠️ Physical Manifestations

  • Acute Heart Palpitations: Immediate racing heartbeat and sudden cold sweating sitting down in a surprise quiz.
  • Muscle Tension: Tension headaches or tightness in the chest before a live graded group presentation.

🧠 Psychological Manifestations

  • Hypervigilance & Panic: Sudden mental freezes, cognitive blockages, or high waves of intense panic.
  • Intrusive Worry: Immediate catastrophic thinking ("I am entirely unequipped and will fail this whole course").

Stage 2: Resistance (The Adaption Phase)

The body attempts to cope and remain stable under continuous, long-term academic pressures.

⚠️ Physical Manifestations

  • Altered Sleep & Digestion: Severe insomnia, acid reflux, or regular stomach aches throughout midterms.
  • Elevated Vitals / Caffeine Dependence: Extended restlessness, relying heavily on excessive coffee/energy drinks.

🧠 Psychological Manifestations

  • Irritability & Low Patience: Snapping at peers and teammates during critical group project discussions.
  • Cognitive Fatigue: Recurring brain fog, low concentration spans, and low recall during revision.

Stage 3: Exhaustion (The Burnout Phase)

The structural energy reserves are entirely spent, resulting in physical and emotional crashes.

⚠️ Physical Manifestations

  • Immune System Collapse: Catching intense flus or infections instantly once exam week ends.
  • Chronic Fatigue Syndrome: Ultimate physical collapse where resting provides zero energy recovery.

🧠 Psychological Manifestations

  • Depressive Apathy: Emotional numbness, complete zero motivation, and isolating yourself from peers.
  • Cynicism & Helplessness: Deeply internalizing standard imposter syndrome thoughts ("I cannot do this anymore").
